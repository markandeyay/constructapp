Exactly fourteen, exactly those ids, in that order. `AAVValidator.run_checks` raises if the
produced list ever differs, and `tests/aav/test_checks.py::TestTheCheckList` asserts it.

`CheckResult.remediation`.

`tier` marks the section 3.4 tier: "A" on PASS, "B" on WARN, None on FAIL and UNKNOWN.

### What is configurable, and how

Every value in the table above, plus the section 6.3 `packaging_limit_bp` request override. The
override replaces the *target* of the band matching the design's modality and moves the soft and
hard limits with it, keeping the default band widths, so an override to 5,000 bp gives
(5,000 / 5,200 / 5,500) rather than a target above its own soft limit. The behaviour is reported
in `parameters_used` as `applied_target_bp`, `applied_soft_limit_bp` and `applied_hard_limit_bp`,
and stated in the length budget's `limit_basis`.

## The ITR flip and flop problem, and how checks 2 and 3 handle it

WP-02 reported that the reverse complement of the right ITR matches the left ITR at 128 of 145
positions, 88.3 percent. Measuring it exactly showed the mechanism, which is cleaner than
sequence divergence and makes both checks exact rather than threshold-dependent.

Writing `core` for the 125 bp A, B, B', C, C', A' hairpin region and `D` for the 20 bp D sequence:

```
itr.aav2_itr_left  == core + D
itr.aav2_itr_right == reverse_complement(D) + core        (holds character for character)
reverse_complement(core) vs core: 0.859 identity          (the flip and flop arm arrangements)
hamming(left, reverse_complement(right)) == 17            (so 128 of 145, 88.3 percent)
```

The same 125 bases of `core` appear in both records. The naive comparison is therefore
`(core + D)` against `(reverse_complement(core) + D)`, and it differs at exactly the 17 positions
where `core` and its own reverse complement differ. That is the whole of the 88.3 percent, and it
is why a check that demands 95 percent identity from that comparison rejects every valid design.
All of this is pinned in `tests/aav/test_itr.py::TestTheFlipFlopFinding`.

**Check 2, `aav.itr_present_both`.** Each ITR in the design is aligned against every reference ITR
of the requested serotype, in both orientations, and the best match is taken. Both registry ITRs
score 1.000 against their own records. Which reference record a copy derives from, and which arm
arrangement it carries, is not a design error. The check never compares one ITR with the reverse
complement of the other. An unregistered serotype is UNKNOWN with the registered ones named, never
a pass.

**Check 3, `aav.itr_orientation`.** Decided from the D element, which is byte identical between the
two records and so unaffected by the arm arrangement. The D sequence occurs at one end of each ITR
only, and in an inverted pair both D sequences face inward: the 5' ITR carries D at its 3' end and
the 3' ITR carries the reverse complement of D at its 5' end. A tandem pair has both at the same
end. This is an exact test with no identity threshold doing real work, and it works equally for the
commoner plasmid layout where both ITRs are the same 145 bp sequence with one reverse complemented.

The D anchor is self checked before it is trusted: `serotype_itr_references` accepts it only if
the reverse complement of the left reference's terminal 20 bases equals the right reference's
leading 20 bases exactly, which confirms the two records really are the two ends of one inverted
terminal repeat pair. If the anchor is unavailable, or the D element cannot be located in both
ITRs (a truncated ITR, for example), the check falls back to an identity margin method: inverted
is called when the 5' ITR is closer to the reverse complement of the 3' ITR than to the 3' ITR as
written, by at least `ITR_ORIENTATION_MARGIN`. If neither wins by the margin the result is UNKNOWN
with the two scores and the margin in the message, never a guess.

## The remediation engine

`packages/validation/aav/remediation.py`, section 6.6 steps 1 through 5.

1. Overage is `total - target`, against the band that the calling check quotes. Check 1 passes
   `self_complementary=False` and check 7 passes `True`, so each message's numbers are internally
   consistent.
2. For every substitutable element (5' enhancer, promoter, intron, WPRE, polyA) the registry is
   enumerated for parts in the same category that are shorter, share at least one
   `host_compatibility` value with the current part, and whose `tissue_specificity` is accepted for
   the design's `target_tissue` by the same configurable table check 8 uses. Substituting a
   promoter for one that would itself raise check 8 is not a fix. The three optional roles of
   section 6.2 (enhancer, intron, WPRE) also get a removal option.
3. bp saved per candidate.
4. Every combination of at most `REMEDIATION_MAX_CHANGES` changes, at most one per element, is
   enumerated; those that close the overage are ranked and the top `REMEDIATION_MAX_PLANS` are
   reported. Ranking is `(number of changes, promoter_preference violated, minus total saved,
   lexical key)`. The first two keys are section 6.6 step 4 verbatim. The third is a documented
   choice: among equally small plans that respect the user's promoter, the one with the most
   headroom leads, which matches the worked example in section 6.6 where the largest single
   substitution is the headline and the smaller alternatives follow. The fourth exists only so the
   order is total and the output is byte stable.
5. When nothing closes the gap the report says so explicitly, gives the largest saving available,
   the remaining deficit, and the longest coding sequence that would fit once every saving is
   applied, and suggests a shorter transgene variant or a dual vector approach.

The engine never proposes changing the ITRs (required in cis, and the registry carries one
reference pair per serotype, so there is nothing shorter) or the transgene (that is the payload,
and shortening it is step 5's separate, quantified suggestion).

It also reports `preference_preserving_saving_bp`, so that when the user named a promoter and no
plan can keep it, the message says so with the numbers rather than silently overriding the choice.

The readable form goes in `CheckResult.message` (section 6.5 and section 5.4 rule 2). The
structured form goes in `CheckResult.remediation` as pipe separated `key=value` records, so the
gold harness can assert a specific substitution and its bp without matching prose.

**Note for WP-06.** The section 9.3 example writes `must_also_report: ["remediation_suggestion"]`.
The field WP-02 added is named `remediation`, and `CheckResult` forbids extra fields, so a gold
case must use `must_also_report: ["remediation"]` or the object form
`{"check": "aav.packaging_limit", "severity": "fail"}`. Filed as a cross-WP request in
`PROGRESS.md`.

## Biological judgment calls, named for WP-10

1. **Check 9 scopes itself to the region between the ITRs.** The two reference ITRs share their
   125 bp hairpin core verbatim, so the ITR pair of every correct cassette is an exact 125 bp
   direct repeat on the plus strand. Counting it would put a WARN on every valid design, which is
   the section 3.4 failure mode: it trains the user to ignore the validator. The ITR relationship
   is policed by checks 2, 3 and 14 instead, and a repeat between an ITR and the interior is still
   caught by check 14. The scope is stated in every check 9 message.
2. **`TISSUE_COMPATIBILITY` is strict by default**: a promoter is compatible only if annotated for
   exactly the target tissue or annotated ubiquitous. hSyn1 does drive expression in retinal
   neurons, so a strict table means a `retina` target with hSyn1 is a WARN. That is deliberate:
   the severity is WARN and never FAIL, the user is the one who knows the intended cell type, and
   widening the table is a one line override.
3. **Check 1 always uses the single stranded band and check 7 the halved one.** Section 6.4 lists
   them as separate checks and section 9.4 asks for a known-bad case where an scAAV cassette sized
   legally for ssAAV fails under the halved capacity, with the gold harness requiring no unexpected
   FAIL. Splitting them this way makes that case report exactly one failure, on the check that
   names the reason. For an scAAV design, check 1 passing means "this length would be fine as an
   ssAAV genome", which the message says.
4. **A D-outward ITR pair is PASS with a note.** Reverse complementing both ITRs in place, without
   swapping them, leaves them inverted relative to each other but with the D sequences facing the
   cassette ends rather than the transgene. Check 3's stated scope is inverted against tandem, and
   this is inverted, so failing it would exceed the check's remit. The message says the arrangement
   is unusual and worth confirming.
5. **Promoter and polyA auto-selection picks the most compact compatible part.** Section 6.7 asks
   for the shortest compatible promoter when `self_complementary` is set and the shortest
   functional polyA when capacity is tight; this applies that rule unconditionally. The reason is
   that the registry quantifies length exactly and does not quantify promoter strength at all, so
   ranking on strength would mean inventing an order, which section 3.3 constraint 1 forbids. The
   longer alternatives are listed in `AAVDesign.notes` with their bp, and `promoter_preference` and
   `polya_preference` override. An exact `tissue_specificity` match always outranks a ubiquitous
   part regardless of length.
6. **An incompatible `promoter_preference` is kept, not substituted.** Section 6.7 reads "the
   user's preference if given and compatible", which could be read as silently substituting. This
   keeps the user's part, records why in `AAVDesign.notes`, and lets check 8 report the WARN it
   exists to produce. Logged as a spec challenge in `PROGRESS.md`.
7. **No transgene is ever invented.** With no `transgene_sequence` and no configured resolver the
   composer raises `TransgeneUnavailable` naming what to do. Section 6.3 allows retrieval by name
   and `AAVComposer(transgene_resolver=...)` is the hook, but this build ships no transgene corpus
   and a validator must not need a database, so a placeholder is not an option: the output is
   exported as orderable DNA.

## Things WP-10 should know about the data

- `polya.synthetic_short` from Appendix B does not exist in the registry. WP-02 could not source a
  real sequence and correctly omitted it. SV40 at 135 bp is the smallest polyA, and nothing in this
  package references a synthetic short polyA. Asserted in
  `tests/aav/test_remediation.py::TestRealPartLengths::test_there_is_no_synthetic_short_polya`.
- Real part lengths differ from Appendix B, and the validator measures the real sequence: CMV 663
  against about 600, CAG 1,639 against about 1,700, GFAP 691 against about 680, WPRE 589 against
  about 600, chimeric intron 141 against about 130. Every number in every message comes from the
  registry.
- `promoter.mecp2_mini` is the registry's lowest confidence source (a patent sequence record). It is
  the default auto-selected promoter for a `cns_neuron` target, because it is the shorter of the two
  annotated neuronal promoters at 225 bp against hSyn1's 472 bp. The `/v1/aav/parts` response
  carries its notes verbatim, including that flag.
- `promoter.cag` carries a 14 bp G run and `promoter.cbh` a 16 bp G run, so check 10 WARNs for any
  cassette using them. `promoter.cmv`, `promoter.cag` and `promoter.cbh` each carry the 21 bp
  sequence `ACGGTAAATGGCCCGCCTGGC` twice (the repeated CMV enhancer motif), so check 9 WARNs for
  those too. Both are genuine Tier B outcomes on standard parts, and both messages say so.

## Outputs, section 6.8

`AAVDesigner.design` returns a `DesignResult` with four inline artifacts: `genbank`, `fasta`,
`length_budget` and `linear_map_json`, plus the `ValidationReport`.

The GenBank record is written with `topology = linear` and every element as a feature carrying
`construct_role` and `construct_part_id`, with the ITRs as `repeat_region`, the promoter and polyA
as `regulatory` with the matching `regulatory_class`, and the transgene as `CDS` with
`transl_table=1`. Provenance is in the record comment. The circular renderer in
`packages/application/exports.py` is not reused, per sections 6.8 and 10.2.

The length budget table gives element, part id, bp, running total and headroom per row, plus a
total row and the band that was applied and why. `linear_map_json` gives the same layout with
zero-based and 1-based coordinates, each element's fraction of the cassette so the UI can draw to
scale, and `topology: "linear"` so a consumer cannot mistake it for a plasmid map.
