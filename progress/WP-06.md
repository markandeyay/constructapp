## 2. Where the sequences come from

Section 3.3 constraint 1 and the WP-04 precedent: prefer slices of real GenBank records already
in the repository, record the offsets, and never present a synthetic sequence as a real part or a
real gene.

| Source | Used by | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `data/parts/**` (14 records) | AAV, assembly | The WP-02 part registry. Every AAV cassette element except the payload is a registry part by id. Assembly fragments are plus strand slices of the same records at recorded offsets. |
| pUC19, GenBank L09137.1, `tests/fixtures/parser/puc19.gb` | AAV payloads, assembly fragments, every gRNA target | A real 2,686 bp record. Its beta-lactamase reading frame lies on the minus strand, ATG at minus strand index 200, TAA ending at 1061, 861 bp including the stop. |
| `packages/validation/grna/vectors.py` | gRNA delivery constructs | The pX330 sense-oligo overhang CACC, the G the human U6 promoter needs at position 1, and the 5' end of the sgRNA scaffold. Real vector context, read from the module rather than recalled. |

### AAV payloads

Four in-frame pieces of the real beta-lactamase reading frame are used, each a clean open reading
frame with an ATG start, a length divisible by three and a single terminal stop:

| Name | bp | Starts | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| `bla` | 861 | ATGAG | the complete reading frame |
| `orf405` | 405 | ATGGG | an in-frame 3' segment |
| `orf312` | 312 | ATGGC | an in-frame 3' segment |
| `orf237` | 237 | ATGGA | an in-frame 3' segment |

The three segments begin ATGG, which matters: `aav.kozak_context` requires G at +4, and +4 is the
fourth base of the coding sequence. The complete reading frame begins ATGA and therefore warns,
which is Tier B case b1 rather than a problem.

Three AAV known-bad cases need a cassette of an exact length that no real payload supplies. Their
payload is a length-calibrated tandem concatenation of whole in-frame copies of the same real
reading frame with one terminal stop codon. In-frame concatenation of a stop-free coding segment
cannot create an in-frame stop, so the open reading frame stays clean and the only thing the case
is about is the length. Every one of those rationales says in terms that the payload is not a real
gene.

Six AAV known-bad cases carry a declared edit to real sequence: a substituted stop codon, one base
removed, a 60 bp in-frame duplication, twelve in-frame adenines, a 21 bp ITR motif inserted in
frame, and 21 transversions in an ITR. Each edit is named in the rationale with its position and
its purpose, and everything around it is untouched.

### The one synthetic target in the whole set

`grna.bad.cas12a_scored_with_a_cas9_pam_offset` and `grna.bad.cas12a_pam_fourth_base_is_t` share a
synthetic 57 bp target. The case needs a position where a real TTTV PAM sits 5' of a 23 nt
protospacer AND a second TTTV sits immediately 3' of it, because the point is that the wrong PAM
still satisfies the motif and is caught on position alone. No position in pUC19 has both. The
target is the minimum needed to isolate the defect, a left flank, a TTTA PAM, 23 nt of spacer, a
decoy TTTC, a right flank, and both rationales say so.

Two gRNA spacers carry a declared substitution into the real target window: six bases replaced by
the BbsI site (Tier B b4) and a synthetic stem spacer (known-bad self-complementarity). A stem that
long does not occur in any real guide in this record.

## 3. AAV

### Tier A, 10 cases

Every Tier A AAV cassette uses `promoter.gfap`. That is forced by the registry rather than chosen.
`aav.kozak_context` needs a purine three bases before the ATG, and that base belongs to whichever
element sits immediately upstream of the coding sequence. Of the eight registry promoters, only
`cag`, `gfap` and `mecp2_mini` end with a purine in that position; `cag` carries a 14 bp G run and
a duplicated CMV enhancer motif that warn on their own; `mecp2_mini` at 225 bp cannot lift any
available real payload over the 2,000 bp minimum genome size. `gfap` is therefore the only
promoter in this registry that can produce a warning-free AAV cassette from real parts and a real
reading frame. **This is a finding about the registry, not a defect in the validator**, and it is
reported to WP-02 under Cross-WP requests: a registry promoter that is both compact and ends with
a purine at -3 would widen the Tier A space considerably.

Within that constraint the ten cases vary the polyA (bGH and SV40), the payload length (237, 312
and 405 bp), the modality (single stranded and self complementary), the input arm (`AAVRequest`
left to the composer, and a fully spelled out `AAVDesign`), and the `packaging_limit_bp` override.
Two of them, a4 at 2,032 bp and a9 at 2,017 bp, sit 32 bp and 17 bp above the minimum genome size,
so they are the guard against that floor drifting upward.

## 4. Assembly

### Determinism: why `hairpin_engine` is pinned to `window` in every case

ViennaRNA is installed in this environment and is preferred by section 7.4 and open question Q6,
but it is an optional import and WP-04's cross-WP request to add it to `requirements.txt` is still
open. On `auto` the `primer.hairpin` verdict therefore depends on whether the package happens to be
installed, and a gold set whose verdicts move with the environment is not a gold set. Every
assembly case pins `hairpin_engine: "window"`, the documented fallback, so the same input gives the
same verdict everywhere. Recorded as a finding for WP-04 and WP-11 below.

### Tier A, 10 cases

Clean designs exist under the window engine for all three strategies, found by sweeping real
slices rather than by relaxing a threshold: three Gibson pairs, four Golden Gate assemblies across
BsaI, BsmBI and BbsI, and three PCR cloning designs including two single-fragment ones. Fifteen
checks apply to a Gibson design, sixteen to Golden Gate and eleven to PCR cloning, and all
applicable checks pass in each. Several cases share `frag_a`, a 150 bp slice of the CBh record at
offset 100, because it is the anchor the sweep found first; the second fragment differs in every
case.

### Tier B, 6 cases

| Case | Documented warning | Justification in one line |
|---|---|---|
| `b1.gibson_window_engine_stem` | `primer.hairpin` | A Gibson primer is a 40 to 47 nt oligo that must begin exactly at the junction, and a 4 bp stem occurs by chance in almost every oligo that long; the free-energy engine reports no significant hairpin for the same primers. |
| `b2.golden_gate_gc_rich_overhang` | `gg.overhang_composition` | A scarless Type IIS join takes its overhang from the downstream fragment's own first four bases, and this junction falls in a GC rich stretch; the alternatives are to move the junction or to leave a scar. |
| `b3.small_insert_amplicon` | `assembly.amplicon_size` | A 60 bp amplicon is an ordinary small part, a tag or a linker; the floor is advice about telling the product from primer dimer on a gel. |
| `b4.fixed_boundary_three_prime_base` | `primer.three_prime_stability` | The primer ends where the fragment ends, and the fragment boundary is the thing being cloned; moving it two bases would amplify something else. |
| `b5.gc_rich_promoter_primer` | `primer.gc_content` | Regulatory elements are frequently GC rich; the 40 to 60 percent window is a recommendation, and the 20 to 80 percent window is the one that describes what can be made. |
| `b6.polya_homopolymer_run` | `primer.homopolymer` | The run is in the bGH polyA part itself, so amplifying the real part means amplifying its homopolymer. |

11. `primer_tm_far_outside_window` more than 10 C from target, which is the FAIL band of check 1;

12. `primer_binds_nowhere_in_template` designing against the wrong version of a sequence; check 9

13. `gibson_overlap_melts_below_floor` a real AT rich arm inside the 15 to 40 bp window that melts

14. `ambiguous_fragment_order` one enzyme at both ends of a PCR clone, so the insert can go in

## 5. Guide RNA

### Targets

Every gRNA target is a real window of pUC19, GenBank L09137.1, except the one synthetic Cas12a
target described in section 2. The coding sequence annotation is always the real beta-lactamase
reading frame, truncated by the window rather than invented: in a window starting at minus strand
A, the part of the frame inside it runs from `max(0, 200 - A)` to `min(window end, 1061) - A`.

Two honest notes are carried in the rationales of the cases they apply to:

* `grna.bad.guide_in_the_final_exon_for_a_knockout` supplies `final_exon_region`. pUC19 is a
  bacterial plasmid and its beta-lactamase gene has no exons, so that span is an annotation
  supplied by the case to exercise the rule. The DNA is real; the exon boundary is a test
  annotation and the rationale says so rather than implying otherwise.
* `grna.bad.homopolymer_run_in_spacer` uses a plus strand window and a knock-in intent. Every
  homopolymer-bearing guide inside the beta-lactamase gene has a T run, and a run of five T
  contains TTTT, so `grna.u6_terminator` would fail alongside it and the case would not be about
  one check. For a knock-in, section 8.5 check 7 passes with its reason stated, so no coding
  sequence annotation is supplied or invented.

### Tier A, 10 cases

Eight forward strand SpCas9 guides from the beta-lactamase reading frame, one reverse strand guide
declared with `strand: -1`, and one SaCas9 guide with a 21 nt spacer and an NNGRRT PAM cloned into
pX601, which declares BsaI so `grna.restriction_in_spacer` evaluates rather than reporting UNKNOWN.
The reverse strand case exists because section 8.3 requires both strands to be scanned and a
forward-only set would never notice if that broke. All ten report PASS on all ten checks.

### Tier B, 5 cases

| Case | Documented warning | Justification in one line |
|---|---|---|
| `b1.alternative_nag_pam` | `grna.pam_valid` | Appendix C names NAG as a tolerated WARN-level alternative; when the edit has to land within a few bases of a position there may be no NGG in range, and a lower-efficiency guide in the right place beats a good one in the wrong place. |
| `b2.u6_motif_under_t7_transcription` | `grna.u6_terminator` | TTTT terminates RNA polymerase III, not T7, so under in vitro transcription the motif is harmless; the warning is real forward-looking information for the move into a U6 plasmid. |
| `b3.gc_rich_spacer` | `grna.gc_content` | In a GC rich stretch every available guide is GC rich, and the cut position is dictated by the edit; the response is to read the off-target report, not to abandon the guide. |
| `b4.cloning_site_inside_the_spacer` | `grna.restriction_in_spacer` | With annealed-oligo cloning the oligos are never digested, so an internal BbsI site is harmless on that route and matters only on re-amplification or reuse. |
| `b5.cut_upstream_of_the_coding_sequence` | `grna.position_in_cds` | Cutting immediately upstream of the initiator region to remove the start codon is a real knockout strategy for genes with internal alternative starts. |

One line of reason per case:

1. `no_pam_present` the real triplet 3' of the protospacer is neither NGG nor NAG, so the nuclease
   does not bind at all; only check 1 asks both whether the PAM matches the motif and whether the
   target carries it at the required offset.
2. `spacer_length_wrong_for_the_nuclease` a 20 nt spacer submitted for SaCas9, which needs 21; the
   spacer looks entirely normal and the PAM is a genuine NNGRRT, so only check 2 can see it.
3. `u6_terminator_in_spacer` polymerase III terminates inside the spacer, so the guide is not made;
   FAIL rather than WARN because the plasmid sequences correctly and nothing works.
4. `guide_targets_its_own_delivery_plasmid` the construct carries the real target site with its real
   PAM, as it would with a knock-in donor, so the nuclease linearises its own plasmid.
5. `close_off_target_in_the_searched_space` one mismatch at the PAM distal end, so the whole seed is
   matched and the site is cut; nothing about the spacer is wrong, so only the off-target search
   can see it.
6. `cas12a_scored_with_a_cas9_pam_offset` the Appendix C integration bug: the supplied PAM satisfies
   TTTV perfectly and is simply on the wrong side, so a motif-only check would pass it.
7. `guide_in_the_final_exon_for_a_knockout` a last-exon frameshift escapes nonsense mediated decay
   and often keeps most of the protein, so the construct looks edited and is not a knockout.
8. `gc_far_outside_the_window` an AT rich spacer forms a weak, short lived duplex, so the edit may
   not be recovered.
9. `homopolymer_run_in_spacer` a run over the Appendix C maximum is synthesised less accurately and
   pairs less specifically.
10. `self_complementary_spacer` a spacer that pairs with itself competes with the scaffold fold and
    with target binding. The threshold is a documented Construct convention, not a published value,
    and the result is a stem length and not a free energy; both limits are stated.
11. `moderate_off_target_with_seed_mismatches` three mismatches, all inside the seed, so section
    8.6's position weighting makes it a WARN; paired with case 5 it would collapse if the weighting
    were ever flattened.
12. `spacer_one_nucleotide_short_for_spcas9` the off-by-one, with `spacer_start` supplied so the
    mis-registered placement is forced rather than inferred.
13. `cas12a_pam_fourth_base_is_t` TTTT is not TTTV, because the IUPAC code V excludes T; the

## 6. Every use of `allowed_extra_fail`, and why

Four uses in 45 known-bad cases. Each is a genuine cascade, where one defect is correctly named by
two checks that say different useful things, and each weakens only the third assertion for the one
check named.

| Case | Allowed | Why it is one defect and not two |
|---|---|---|
| `aav.bad.no_polya` | `aav.polya_present_functional` | Removing the polyA element trips check 4, which confirms the three required elements are present, and check 12, which asks whether the polyA present is a recognised functional signal. | `assembly.bad.two_identical_overhangs` | `assembly.fragment_order_defined` | Identical overhangs are precisely what makes the assembly order ambiguous. WP-04's own unit test allows the same pair. |
| `assembly.bad.two_gibson_junctions_share_homology` | `assembly.fragment_order_defined` | The same relationship through the Gibson path: shared homology between two junctions is what admits more than one arrangement. |
| `grna.bad.guide_targets_its_own_delivery_plasmid` | `grna.off_target_hits` | Under the `construct_only` scope the delivery construct is inside the searched off-target space by definition, so a cut site in the construct is necessarily both a self-targeting hit and a near-perfect off-target hit. Everywhere else the list is empty, including the eight known-bad cases that carry additional WARN

## 7. Findings and cross-WP requests

None of these is a validator being wrong, which is worth saying plainly: 91 of 91 cases agree, and

3. **Naming, already ruled on and recorded.** Section 9.3's example writes

## 10. Audit corrections

An independent audit read every known-bad case against its own input and its own report. It found no
validator fault and no wrong verdict: the gate was green before these corrections and is green after
them, at 91 of 91 with zero disagreements. What it found was text that did not match data. Section
9.3 makes the rationale load bearing, so a case that fails for an unstated reason, or whose stated
reason is false, proves less than it appears to. Every finding below was re-verified here against
the records in `data/parts/**` and `tests/fixtures/parser/puc19.gb` and against the live report,
then fixed. Where a fix changed an input, the case was re-run and still fails on its named check at
its named severity with no unexpected FAIL.

### 10.1 False provenance, the two most serious

A false sequence attribution in a gold case is the same class of error the product exists to
prevent, which is why these two are listed first.

**`assembly.bad.primer_binds_nowhere_in_template`**

* Claimed: the forward primer is "a 20 bp slice of GenBank AF396260.1, CMV promoter (part
  promoter.cmv) at offset 502".
* Actually true: `ACGTACGTAAGGTTCCAAGG` occurs in no record in this repository, on either strand.
  `promoter.cmv[502:522]` is `ATGTCGTAACAACTCCGCCC`, which is what the fragment really carries at
  offset 40.
* Changed: the fragment `source` field and the rationale now declare the primer deliberately
  synthetic and say why a real slice would contradict the defect under test instead of expressing
  it, and both name the real slice that sits where the primer record claims its site. Everything
  else in the case was verified real: flanks are `promoter.cmv[462:502]` and `promoter.cmv[582:632]`,
  the interior is `promoter.cbh[572:632]`, and the reverse primer is `puc19[330:350]`.

**`assembly.bad.primer_tm_far_outside_window`**

* Claimed, in both the rationale and the fragment `source`: the primer is "AT rich" and "far below
  the target melting temperature".
* Actually true: the binding region `TGGCCCGCCTGGCTGACC` is 18 bp and 14 of those bases are G or C,
  78% GC, and the validator reports 71.9 C, which is 11.9 C **above** the 60.0 C target. The slice
  itself is a genuine `promoter.cbh[21:39]`, so that half of the provenance was sound.
* Changed: both strings corrected to GC rich and above target, with the measured numbers, and the
  two consequential warnings declared (see 10.4).

**`grna.bad.cut_outside_the_coding_sequence_for_a_knockout`**

* Actually true: spacer at target index 154, cut site 171, `cds_region` [231, 911]. The cut was

* Changed: `cds_region` moved to [50, 150], the first 100 bases of the real reading frame, so the

  boundary rather than one side twice. The rationale states the narrowing as a case annotation,
  exactly as b5 states its own.

**`grna.bad.cas12a_pam_fourth_base_is_t`**

* Actually true: `TTTT` occurs nowhere in the 61 bp target on either strand, and the four bases

### 10.5 The degenerate case

`grna.bad.guide_in_the_final_exon_for_a_knockout` set `final_exon_region` to [50, 911], identical to
`cds_region`. Declaring the whole CDS to be the final exon means the case cannot distinguish the
final-exon branch from "anywhere in the CDS", so it tested nothing specific: deleting the final-exon
clause from the validator would not have made the case fail.

It now uses a strict subset. `final_exon_region` is [520, 911], 45% of the real 50 to 911 reading
frame, and the guide is the real protospacer at window offset 523 with its own real `CGG` PAM,
cutting at target position 540. That is 20 bp inside the final exon and 57% into the coding sequence.

The 57% is load bearing. `knockout_cds_max_fraction` is 0.60, so an isolated final-exon case needs a
cut that is inside the final exon **and** inside the first 60% of the frame at the same time. With
`cds_region` [50, 911] that caps the cut at position 566, which in turn means the declared final
exon cannot span much less than 40% of the coding sequence. Any smaller exon and the check reports a
second clause about cutting too late, and the case stops isolating the branch it names. That
arithmetic is now in the rationale, along with the unchanged honest note that pUC19 is bacterial, its
beta-lactamase gene has no exons, and the exon boundary is a test annotation and nothing more.
