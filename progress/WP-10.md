## 2. RULING: the sliding-window hairpin threshold

**Question put to this review.** Is a 4 bp stem closing a 3 nt loop a meaningful
hairpin risk in a tailed assembly primer, or is `HAIRPIN_WINDOW_STEM_WARN_BP = 4`
simply too strict for that primer class?

**Answer: the threshold is too strict, by a wide and measurable margin, and a
4 bp stem closing a minimal loop is not a hairpin at all in the thermodynamic
sense. Raise the WARN stem to 6 bp and the 3' FAIL stem to 7 bp.** The reasoning
is below, and a separate and more serious defect in the same function is
reported with it.

### 2.1 The physics: a 4 bp stem on a minimal loop does not fold

A hairpin's folding free energy is the stem's stacking free energy plus a
positive loop initiation penalty. For DNA at 37 C the mean nearest-neighbor
stacking term is about -1.3 kcal/mol per stack, and a stem of k base pairs
contributes k - 1 stacks. The hairpin loop initiation penalty for a 3 to 6 nt
DNA loop is about +4.5 kcal/mol, and the 3 nt triloop is the most penalized loop
there is.

| Stem | Stacks | Stacking dG37 | Loop penalty | Net dG37 | Against the -2.0 WARN |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 bp | 3 | about -3.9 | +4.5 | about **+0.6** | not folded at all |
| 5 bp | 4 | about -5.2 | +4.5 | about **-0.7** | folded but well short of WARN |
| 6 bp | 5 | about -6.5 | +4.5 | about **-2.0** | **this is the WARN threshold** |
| 7 bp | 6 | about -7.8 | +4.5 | about **-3.3** | past the -3.0 FAIL level |

A 4 bp stem closing a 3 nt loop has a *positive* folding free energy at 37 C, and
a PCR annealing step runs at 50 to 72 C where it is more unfavourable still. It
is not a competing structure; it is a geometric coincidence. This is exactly why
the same primers are clean under ViennaRNA: ViennaRNA is right and the window
score is reporting a shape, not a structure.

That table also supplies the principled calibration the current constant lacks.
The two engines are supposed to express the same constraint in different units.
**6 bp is the stem length whose folding free energy equals the
`HAIRPIN_DG_WARN_KCAL_PER_MOL = -2.0` the ViennaRNA path uses, and 7 bp is the
stem length at the `HAIRPIN_THREE_PRIME_DG_FAIL_KCAL_PER_MOL = -3.0` FAIL level.**
Setting the window thresholds to 6 and 7 makes the fallback agree in severity
with the engine it is standing in for, instead of contradicting it.

### 2.2 The measurement: the current threshold has no specificity at this length

I measured `window_hairpin` (the shipped function, imported unmodified) on random
oligos at three GC contents, 2,000 replicates per cell, `min_loop_nt=3`,
`three_prime_window_nt=5`. The figure is the fraction of oligos whose best stem
reaches k bp, that is, the rate at which the check fires on sequence with no
structure designed into it at all.

| Oligo length | stem >= 3 | **stem >= 4 (current WARN)** | stem >= 5 (current 3' FAIL) | stem >= 6 | stem >= 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 nt | 0.65 | **0.19** | 0.04 | 0.006 | 0.001 |
| 25 nt | 0.88 | **0.34** | 0.08 | 0.014 | 0.002 |
| 30 nt | 0.96 | **0.51** | 0.14 | 0.032 | 0.006 |
| 40 nt | 0.998 | **0.79** | 0.30 | 0.080 | 0.022 |
| 45 nt | 1.000 | **0.88** | 0.39 | 0.106 | 0.027 |
| 47 nt | 1.000 | **0.90** | 0.41 | 0.111 | 0.025 |
| 60 nt | 1.000 | **0.98** | 0.63 | 0.213 | 0.049 |

**At the 40 to 47 nt length of a tailed assembly primer the current WARN fires on
about nine random oligos in ten.** A check that fires on 90 percent of
structureless sequence carries no information. This is the section 3.4 failure
mode named in the specification: it trains the user to ignore the validator.

The measurement also shows precisely where WP-04's reasoning went wrong, and it
is worth stating because the reasoning was not careless. `constants.py` line 184
justifies 4 as: "Three pairs closing a 3 nt loop is the shortest fold the
geometry allows and occurs by chance in most 20-mers, so flagging at 3 would flag
everything." That is **correct for a 20-mer**: 3 bp fires at 0.65 and 4 bp at
0.19 in a 20-mer. The defect is that a bare base-pair count is not
length-normalized, and the same constant is then applied to a 47-mer where 4 bp
has become the "flags everything" value. The threshold was calibrated for one
primer class and applied to another.

One framing correction for the record: the brief describes the problem as "a 4 bp
stem closing a 3 nt loop." Raising the minimum loop will **not** fix it. In the
measured 47-mers only about 5 percent of the qualifying stems close a 3 nt loop;
the loop sizes are spread across the whole available range. A rule of stem >= 5
with loop >= 4 fires at 0.40 against stem >= 5 alone at 0.42. The excess comes
from stem length, not from minimal loops, so the loop parameter is not the lever.

### 2.3 This is not only noise. It is producing FAILs on correct designs.

The reported problem is a WARN, but `HAIRPIN_WINDOW_STEM_THREE_PRIME_FAIL_BP = 5`
is a **FAIL**, and a 5 bp stem touching the last 5 bases of a 47-mer occurs in
**13.4 percent** of random oligos at 50 percent GC. Roughly one tailed primer in
seven is at risk of an outright hairpin FAIL on a design with nothing wrong with
it, with no way for the designer to move the oligo off its junction. That is
materially worse than a noisy WARN and it is the part of this finding with bench
consequences.

### 2.4 ERROR, separately: a 3'-anchored FAIL can be masked by a longer internal stem

**ERROR.** File `packages/validation/assembly/structure.py`, `window_hairpin`,
lines 248-275. Source contradicted: the specification's own section 7.5 check 6,
which requires FAIL when the 3' end lies in a stem at or past the FAIL threshold.

`window_hairpin` returns a single `HairpinResult`. The selection rule at line 264
is `stem > best.stem_bp or (stem == best.stem_bp and involved and not
best.three_prime_involved)`: stem length dominates, and 3' involvement breaks ties
only at **equal** stem length. A primer that has both a long internal stem and a
shorter but 3'-anchored stem therefore reports the internal one, and the
3'-anchored condition that check 6 specifies as FAIL is never seen.

Measured, 1,200 replicates at 50 percent GC, counting oligos where a stem at or
past the FAIL threshold does touch the 3' window but the reported result does not
have `three_prime_involved` set:

| Oligo length | True 3'-FAIL condition present | Reported as FAIL | **Missed** | Share of true FAILs missed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 40 nt | 0.108 | 0.104 | 0.004 | 5 of 130, 3.8 percent |
| 47 nt | 0.134 | 0.127 | 0.007 | 9 of 161, 5.6 percent |
| 60 nt | 0.160 | 0.139 | 0.021 | 25 of 192, 13.0 percent |

Value found: one result selected by maximum stem length. Value expected: the
severity-maximising result, that is, the longest 3'-involved stem reported
whenever it reaches the FAIL threshold, independently of whether some internal
stem is longer. **This is a false negative on a FAIL-severity check and it is
the opposite of the over-strictness problem, so fixing the threshold does not
fix it.** Under my recommended 6/7 thresholds the masking rate falls to zero in
1,200 replicates at every length tested, so the threshold change happens to
suppress it, but the defect remains latent and should be fixed on its own terms.
Owner: WP-04.

### 2.5 Recommendation, specific

1. **`HAIRPIN_WINDOW_STEM_WARN_BP`: 4 to 6.** Justification to write into the
   docstring is the free-energy equivalence in 2.1, which upgrades the constant
   from CHOSEN DEFAULT to a value anchored on the -2.0 kcal/mol WARN the other
   engine uses, plus the measured base rate in 2.2.
2. **`HAIRPIN_WINDOW_STEM_THREE_PRIME_FAIL_BP`: 5 to 7.** The existing
   "one bp above the WARN stem" relation is sound reasoning and is preserved.
   7 bp is also where the -3.0 kcal/mol FAIL level lands.
3. **Fix the selection rule in `window_hairpin` (2.4) regardless of the
   threshold.** Prefer the 3'-involved stem whenever it reaches the FAIL
   threshold. Returning two results, best-overall and best-3'-anchored, is the
   cleaner shape and costs nothing.
4. **Report which segment of the oligo each stem arm lies in** (fixed tail versus
   template-binding region). The module already knows the binding-region
   boundary, because `tm_measured_on` depends on it. This is what makes a
   surviving hairpin WARN actionable: both arms in the binding region means the
   primer can be walked along the template, whereas an arm inside a Gibson
   homology arm or a Type IIS tail cannot be moved and the user needs to be told
   that rather than left to discover it.
5. **Do not exclude the tail from the hairpin scan.** I considered recommending
   it and I am recommending against it. A hairpin whose 5' arm sits in the tail is
   a real structure that competes with annealing in the early cycles, even though
   it is unactionable, and the 3' terminus can only ever be sequestered by a stem
   whose other arm lies somewhere upstream, often the tail. Suppressing the scan
   there would hide the one hairpin class that genuinely stops a primer working.
   Label the segment, do not drop it.
6. **Longer term, the durable fix is to stop counting base pairs.** Even at a
   threshold of 6 the firing rate still runs from 0.006 at 20 nt to 0.21 at 60 nt
   across the 18 to 60 nt range this module itself designs
   (`PRIMER_MIN_LENGTH_NT` 18, `PRIMER_MAX_TOTAL_LENGTH_NT` 60), so the constant
   is still doing different work at the two ends of its own range. WP-04 already
   ships the SantaLucia stacking table in `packages/core/sequence/tm.py`; the only
   missing piece for a crude stem-stacking-plus-loop-penalty estimate in the
   fallback is a loop penalty term, which would need to be sourced. That would put
   the fallback in the same units as the primary engine and retire the bp count.
   Not required now. Worth recording as the intended direction.

### 2.6 A claim-safety consequence the operator should know

ViennaRNA is **not importable in this environment** (confirmed:
`ModuleNotFoundError: No module named 'RNA'`), so the window engine is the live
path for the demo and for the test suite. Section 16's Determinism row permits
"The same design produces the same verdict." Today the same design produces
`primer.hairpin` WARN on the window engine and PASS on ViennaRNA at a divergence
rate near 90 percent for tailed primers. The engine is named in
`HairpinResult.engine` and in `parameters_used`, which is the honest mitigation
and was the right thing for WP-04 to do, but it does not make the two engines
agree. Recommendations 1 and 2 close the gap, which is a second and independent
reason to adopt them.

### 3.2 The question put to this review

Because the check requires a purine at -3, only some registry promoters can
yield a warning-free cassette. **Is the check reading the upstream context
correctly, or is it an artifact of taking the -3 base from whatever element
precedes the CDS rather than from a Kozak sequence a designer would normally
supply with the transgene?**

**Ruling: the check is reading the correct position and is correct as written. Do
not change the check. The artifact is real, but it lives in the composer, not in
the validator. This is a generation defect reported by a working validator.**

### 3.3 Why the check is reading the right position

It is worth separating two things that are easy to conflate, because the answer
turns on the distinction.

The bases immediately 5' of the ATG in the plasmid **do** become the 5' UTR of
the transcript. The transcription start site lies inside the promoter, upstream
of the promoter part's 3' end, so everything from the TSS through to the ATG is
transcribed and really is positions -1, -2, -3 of the mRNA. Reading
`cassette[start - 3]` is therefore not reading from the wrong molecule or the
wrong coordinate. The check is measuring the true -3 position of the construct as
composed. That part is sound and I want it on the record, because the superficial
reading, that a promoter sequence has "nothing to do with translation", is wrong.

### 3.4 Why the finding is nevertheless an artifact

The position is real. The **value** at that position is an accident.

`AAVComposer` concatenates promoter, optional intron, CDS and polyA with no
initiation-context element between the upstream element and the ATG, and it does
not require the supplied `transgene_sequence` to carry its own 5' context. So the
-3 base is whatever the promoter part's source GenBank feature happened to end
on. It is a consequence of where a curator drew a feature boundary, and it
carries no information whatever about translation initiation.

I measured the 3' end of every element that can sit immediately 5' of the CDS:

| Element | Final 6 bases | Base at -3 | Purine at -3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `promoter.cag` | `GGCAAA` | A | **yes** |
| `promoter.cbh` | `GGTTGG` | T | no |
| `promoter.cmv` | `GCCTCC` | T | no |
| `promoter.ef1a` | `TCGTGA` | T | no |
| `promoter.efs` | `ACACAG` | C | no |
| `promoter.gfap` | `CAAGCT` | G | **yes** |
| `promoter.hsyn1` | `CAGTCG` | T | no |
| `promoter.mecp2_mini` | `CGGAAA` | A | **yes** |
| `intron.chimeric` | `ACCTGC` | T | no |

This confirms the measurement in the brief exactly. **Three of eight promoters
carry a purine at -3, and all three by chance.** Three of eight is 0.375 against
the 0.5 expected from random sequence, which at n = 8 is entirely consistent with
coin-flipping. There is no promoter in this registry for which the -3 base is a
designed feature, and the EFS versus GFAP difference the brief measured is
visible here as nothing more than `ACACAG` against `CAAGCT`.

Two consequences the brief did not name, and both matter:

- **`intron.chimeric` ends in T at -3.** So *every* cassette that includes the
  optional chimeric intron draws the Kozak WARN regardless of which promoter was
  chosen, including GFAP. The intron is the last element before the CDS when it is
  present, so it, not the promoter, sets the -3 base. The three-usable-promoters
  figure collapses to zero usable promoters as soon as the intron is included.
- Of the three promoters that do pass, `promoter.cag` carries a 14 base G run and
  so trips the homopolymer check, and `promoter.mecp2_mini` is the registry's
  lowest-confidence record. **`promoter.gfap` really is the only practical
  warning-free choice, and it is astrocyte-specific**, so the demo is steered to
  an astrocyte target by an accident of feature boundaries. That is a bad place
  for a demo to be steered to by chance.

### 3.5 Recommendation

**Owner: WP-03 (the AAV composer), not the validator.**

1. **The composer should place a Kozak element between the last upstream element
   and the CDS**, unless the supplied transgene already carries 6 or more bases of
   5' context ending in an ATG, in which case the user's context is honoured and
   recorded. The element belongs in the length budget, in the GenBank feature
   table and in `AAVDesign.notes` like every other element, at 6 bp.
2. **Inserting `GCCACC` is not fabrication and section 3.3 constraint 1 does not
   forbid it.** I want to be explicit about this, because these packages have been
   properly conservative about writing sequence down and might otherwise refuse
   the fix on sourcing grounds. `GCCRCC` is a published, cited consensus that is
   already in this codebase at `constants.py` line 251 with its Kozak 1987
   citation. Instantiating `R` as `A`, the preferred purine in that same work,
   and emitting the result as a designed regulatory element with that citation
   attached, is ordinary vector design and is fully sourced. It is categorically
   different from reciting a MoClo overhang set from memory.
3. **Whether or not the composer is changed, the WARN message must say where the
   -3 base came from.** Today it reports "position -3 is T" and advises inserting
   the `GCCRCC` prefix, which is the right advice. What it does not say is that
   the offending T is the last-but-two base of `promoter.efs` and was never
   anything the user chose. A user who assembled a cassette from registry parts
   and is told their initiation context is weak has no way to find out whose base
   that is. Name the upstream element in the message. This is the minimum fix and
   it is cheap.
4. **Do not relax the check and do not widen the accepted bases at -3 to make the
   demo clean.** A purine at -3 is the single best-supported determinant of
   initiation efficiency in the cited work, a weak context genuinely permits
   leaky scanning, and the severity is already only WARN. Section 3.4 cuts in
   both directions: a validator that is quietly loosened to keep a demo green is
   worse than one that reports an inconvenient truth.
5. **For the demo:** adding the Kozak element is strictly better than choosing
   GFAP to dodge the warning, because it makes every promoter yield a clean
   cassette and removes a visible coincidence from the script. It also removes
   the awkward follow-up question, which is that a cassette the tool composed and
   exported as orderable DNA is missing an element that essentially every real
   AAV transgene cassette contains.

### 3.6 One honest caveat on the ruling

The alternative reading, that check 11 should report UNKNOWN rather than WARN
whenever the -3 base comes from an element the user did not supply, is
defensible. I considered it and rejected it. The construct as composed really does
have a weak initiation context, a researcher who ordered it really would get
reduced expression, and UNKNOWN would suppress a true finding about the actual
molecule. WARN with a message that names the source of the base is the correct
behaviour. The defect is that the tool composes a cassette missing an element it
should supply, and the right response to a validator catching your generator is
to fix the generator.

Biologically the band is the right shape and the module's own framing of it is
the honest one. The wild-type AAV2 genome is 4,679 bp in `NC_001401.2`, so a
4,700 bp target is anchored on the real capsid cargo rather than on a round

in the vectorology literature, which is exactly why banding beats asserting.

about half. 2,400 against 4,700 is slightly more than half, which is the right

`AAV_MIN_GENOME_BP` 2,000 as a WARN is also right: under-length genomes do

correct severity.

**A spec inconsistency the implementation resolved, and resolved the right way.**
Section 6.5's inline code block comments `AAV_SS_HARD_LIMIT_BP = 5_200 # over
this: FAIL`, while the "Severity banding for check 1" table immediately below it
says FAIL at `> soft limit`, that is above 4,900. These contradict each other.
`_band_severity` (`checks.py` lines 189-201) takes only `target` and `soft`, so
FAIL begins above 4,900 and the hard limit changes only the message. That
follows the banding table, and the section 6.6 worked example settles it: a
5,048 bp cassette is described as failing and triggering remediation while
sitting 152 bp **under** the 5,200 bp ceiling, which is only possible under the
table's reading. The function's docstring states this reasoning explicitly.
**CONFIRMED as the correct resolution.** Recorded here so that nobody later
"fixes" the code to match the stray comment and silently breaks the section 15.3
demo beat.

holds "out to at least 25 nt". I measure it holding to 40 nt, so the claim is

with the A, B, B', C, C', A', D structure, the A to A' part is the palindromic
hairpin and the roughly 20 nt D sequence is present at only the transgene-facing
end, which is what makes orientation decidable. **CONFIRMED.**

`ITR_IDENTITY_THRESHOLD = 0.95` deserves its own note because the naive
application of it would be a serious bug and the module avoided it. A correct
cassette's 5' ITR compared against the reverse complement of its 3' ITR scores
0.878, below 0.95, so a check that applied the threshold to the **pair** would
FAIL every valid design. The module instead applies 0.95 to each ITR against the
**serotype reference set** in both orientations, where it does real work, and
decides orientation from the D element where no threshold is involved at all.
`aav.itr_orientation` falls back to the identity-margin test only when the D
element cannot be located and returns UNKNOWN rather than guessing when the
margin is not met. **CONFIRMED, and this is the single most likely place a
careless AAV implementation would reject every correct design.**

### 4.3 WP-03 judgment call 1: scoping repeat detection to the region between the ITRs

**RULING: SOUND. Adopt as written.**

The premise is exactly true and I verified it independently: the longest common
substring between `itr.aav2_itr_left` and `itr.aav2_itr_right` on the plus strand
is **exactly 125 bp**, at left offset 0 and right offset 20. Every correct AAV2
cassette therefore contains a 125 bp exact direct repeat, formed by the ITR pair
itself, six times longer than `MAX_DIRECT_REPEAT_BP`. Counting it would put a
`aav.internal_repeats` WARN on 100 percent of valid designs, which is the section
3.4 failure mode by definition.

The scoping is also not a loss of coverage, which is the part that makes the call
sound rather than merely convenient:

- The ITR-to-ITR relationship is policed by checks 2, 3 and 14 instead, which is
  where it belongs, and check 3 decides it from the D element exactly.
- A repeat between an ITR and the cassette interior is still caught, by check 14
  (`aav.itr_internal_sites`), which asks whether any 20 nt window of the serotype
  ITR reference occurs between the ITRs on either strand. That is the
  recombination hazard that actually matters, because an internal ITR copy is a
  substrate for aberrant resolution and truncated genomes.
- The scope is stated in every check 9 message, so the reader is never left to
  infer it.

The only residual I would note for the operator: a direct repeat lying wholly
inside one ITR, or between the two ITRs' own internal sub-repeats, is not
reported. That is correct, because the ITR is a palindrome by design and its
internal structure is the element working as intended.

### 4.4 `MAX_HOMOPOLYMER_RUN = 8`: is it too strict for real promoters?

**RULING: the threshold is right and the two warnings it produces are true
positives, not artifacts. Do not relax it.** This is the opposite conclusion to
the hairpin threshold in section 2, and the contrast is instructive.

I scanned all 14 registry parts for runs longer than 8:

| Part | Runs longer than 8 |
|---|---|
| `promoter.cag` | 14 consecutive G at offset 412 |
| `promoter.cbh` | 16 consecutive G at offset 393 |
| all other 12 parts | none |

Note that `promoter.cag` and `promoter.cbh` also each carry two 8-base C runs,
which do **not** trip the check, because `homopolymer_runs` is called with
`min_length=limit + 1`. The boundary is handled correctly: "longer than 8" means
9 or more, and the message says "longer than 8 bp". **CONFIRMED.**

Why this is not the hairpin situation:

1. **The base rate is 2 parts in 14, not 90 percent.** The check discriminates.
2. **The finding is physically real.** A 14 to 16 base G run is genuinely
   difficult to synthesize, is a candidate G-quadruplex, and is a known source of
   polymerase slippage and sequencing indels. A commercial gene synthesis vendor
   would flag the same stretch. The validator is telling the truth.
3. **The value is SPEC**, fixed by section 6.5, so it is not WP-03's number to
   change.
4. **The severity is WARN and the message handles it correctly.** I read
   `check_homopolymer_runs`: it locates the longest run in the cassette element
   that contains it, names that element, and then says in so many words that if
   the run sits in a registry part "it is expected and documented: promoter.cag
   carries a 14 bp G run and promoter.cbh a 16 bp G run in their GC rich cores.
   This is a Tier B warning, not a failure." That is exactly the section 3.4
   treatment: surfaced, located, explained, not failed. **CONFIRMED.**

**QUESTIONABLE (low severity), `constants.py` line 162.** One threshold is
applied to all four bases. G and C runs are materially more troublesome than A
and T runs of the same length, for synthesis and for quadruplex formation, so a
uniform 8 is slightly lax for G or C and slightly strict for A or T. The value is
spec-fixed so this is not actionable now, and both real trips happen to be G
runs, so the practical effect is nil. Worth knowing if challenged.

### 4.7 Other AAV judgment calls ruled on

WP-03 named seven. Calls 1 (repeat scoping) and the Kozak consequence are ruled
on above. The remaining five:

**Call 2, `TISSUE_COMPATIBILITY` strict by default. SOUND.** A `retina` target
with hSyn1 draws a WARN even though hSyn1 does drive retinal neurons. I endorse
this. The severity is WARN and never FAIL, widening the table is a one-line
override, and the user is the party who knows the intended cell type. The
alternative, a permissive table, would silently bless a promoter and tissue
combination the tool has no evidence for, which is worse. Strict and overridable
beats loose and confident.

**Call 3, check 1 always uses the single-stranded band and check 7 the halved
one. SOUND, and it is the only reading that satisfies section 9.4.** Section 6.4
lists them as separate checks and section 9.4 asks for a known-bad case where an
scAAV cassette sized legally for ssAAV fails the halved capacity, with the gold
harness requiring no unexpected FAIL. Splitting them this way makes that case
report exactly one failure, on the check that names the reason. The PASS message
for check 1 on an scAAV design correctly reads as "this length would be fine as
an ssAAV genome", which is a true statement and not a misleading one.

**Call 4, a D-outward ITR pair is PASS with a note. SOUND but marginal, and the
one I would most want the operator to be ready for.** Reverse complementing both
ITRs in place without swapping them leaves them inverted relative to each other
but with the D sequences facing the cassette ends rather than the transgene.
Check 3's stated scope is inverted against tandem, this arrangement is inverted,
so failing it would exceed the check's remit. That reasoning is correct on its
own terms. The biological reality, though, is that D-outward is not a functional
AAV genome: the D sequence must face the transgene for the terminal resolution
site to be used correctly. So the tool passes a construct that would not work.
The mitigation is that the message says the arrangement is unusual and worth
confirming. **My recommendation is to keep the PASS but raise the note to a WARN
on a separate check id**, because "unusual and worth confirming" inside a PASS
message is the kind of thing a user skims past, and this is a design that wastes
a production run. Owner: WP-03. This is the AAV finding most likely to cost
bench time, and I have ranked it accordingly in section 8.

**Call 5, promoter and polyA auto-selection picks the most compact compatible
part. SOUND, and the reasoning is the best kind.** Section 6.7 asks for the
shortest compatible promoter when `self_complementary` is set; this applies the
rule unconditionally. The justification is the right one: the registry quantifies
length exactly and does not quantify promoter strength at all, so ranking on
strength would mean inventing an order, which section 3.3 constraint 1 forbids.
Listing the longer alternatives with their bp in `AAVDesign.notes`, and
preferring an exact `tissue_specificity` match over a ubiquitous part regardless
of length, together make this a defensible default rather than a hidden
preference. One consequence to be ready for: the compact choice is often the
weaker promoter (EFS against CAG), so a user optimising for expression rather
than for capacity should set `promoter_preference`. The notes say so.

**Call 6, an incompatible `promoter_preference` is kept, not substituted.
SOUND.** Section 6.7's "the user's preference if given and compatible" could be
read as licensing silent substitution. Keeping the user's part, recording why in
the notes, and letting check 8 report the WARN it exists to produce is correct:
silently swapping a user's promoter is the worst available behaviour, because the
exported GenBank would then not be the design they asked for.

**Call 7, no transgene is ever invented. SOUND, and non-negotiable.** With no
`transgene_sequence` and no configured resolver, `TransgeneUnavailable` is raised
naming what to do. The output is exported as orderable DNA; a placeholder
transgene in a file a user can send to a synthesis vendor is the single worst
failure mode this system could have. **CONFIRMED as the right call.**

### 5.3 WP-05 judgment call 9: withholding the MIT score for SaCas9 and Cas12a

**RULING: SOUND, strongly, and I would not accept a change to it.** WP-05 calls
this its most consequential honesty decision. I agree with the decision and I
think it is less costly than WP-05 seems to fear.

Why it is right:

1. **There is no defined mapping.** The weight vector has 20 elements fitted to
   20 nt SpCas9 protospacers. A 21 nt SaCas9 spacer or a 23 nt Cas12a spacer has
   no principled alignment onto it. Padding or truncating, which the reference
   implementation does, invents a position correspondence.
2. **The direction of the error would be unknown.** SaCas9 and Cas12a have
   genuinely different mismatch-tolerance profiles, and Cas12a is widely reported
   to be the more specific enzyme with a different PAM-proximal sensitivity
   pattern. An extrapolated number could be optimistic or pessimistic and the
   user could not tell which. A number that is wrong in an unknown direction is
   worse than no number, because it will be acted on.
3. **The reference implementation itself refuses.** `MIT_DOMAIN` records that the
   tool states this model and the related CFD model were developed for SpCas9 and
   that there is not enough data to support their use for Cas12a, and that it
   scores SaCas9 with a separate published model. So the withholding is not
   WP-05's caution, it is the upstream tool's own documented position.
4. **And the decisive point, which I verified in the code: withholding the score
   costs the user no protection at all.** The off-target severity rule in
   `checks.py` lines 933-934 is `hit.mismatches <= off_target_fail_max_mismatches
   and hit.seed_mismatches <= off_target_fail_max_seed_mismatches`. It does not
   reference the MIT score. So a SaCas9 or Cas12a guide still gets its hits, its
   mismatch positions, its seed mismatch counts, its FAIL or WARN severity and
   its searched-space statement. The only thing missing is a published number. The
   safety behaviour is identical.

That fourth point should be how this is presented, because it converts the
decision from a sacrifice into a free choice. The message already preempts the
misreading: it says the score is withheld and then immediately says every hit is
still reported with its mismatch positions and its seed mismatch count. **CONFIRMED.**

### 5.4 WP-05 judgment call 7: `SEED_REGION_NT = 12`

**RULING: SOUND and within the Appendix C range, but it is the end of the range
that produces the fewest FAILs, and the operator should know that.**

Within Appendix C's stated 8 to 12, 12 is permitted, and `__post_init__` rejects
anything outside the range rather than accepting it silently. On the biology, 12
is well supported, and more strongly than WP-05 argued for it. WP-05's
corroboration was the reference tool's `last12MmCount`. The better evidence is
sitting in the same package: the MIT weight vector itself. Positions 9 through
20 of a 20 nt spacer carry weights of 0.389, 0.079, 0.445, 0.508, 0.613, 0.851,
0.732, 0.828, 0.615, 0.804, 0.685, 0.583, while positions 1 through 5 carry
0.0, 0.0, 0.014, 0.0, 0.0. The empirical mismatch-sensitivity transition really
does sit around position 9 or 10, not position 13. A 12 nt seed matches the
measured weights better than an 8 nt seed does.

**QUESTIONABLE (moderate), and it is a real one.** The FAIL rule is
`mismatches <= 2 AND seed_mismatches <= 0`, so it requires a perfectly matched
seed. A **larger** seed therefore makes the FAIL condition **harder** to satisfy,
not easier. With a 20 nt spacer:

- `seed = 12`: the seed is positions 9 to 20, so a 2-mismatch site FAILs only if
  both mismatches fall in positions 1 to 8.
- `seed = 8`: the seed is positions 13 to 20, so a 2-mismatch site FAILs if both
  mismatches fall anywhere in positions 1 to 12.

So `seed_region_nt = 12` is the **permissive** end of Appendix C's range for the
off-target FAIL rule, and the same holds for the check 8 near-match WARN, which
also keys on a fully matched seed. Choosing the top of the range is defensible on
the weight-vector evidence above, and I am not recommending a change, but the
operator should not be under the impression that the top of the range is the
conservative choice. It is the opposite.

One position where the binary window and the graded weights genuinely disagree:
spacer position 10 carries a weight of only 0.079, meaning a mismatch there is
well tolerated, yet under `seed = 12` it counts as a seed mismatch and therefore
spares a site from FAIL. That is a small, real inconsistency inherent in using a
binary window as a proxy for a graded vector.

**Recommendation, not blocking.** For SpCas9 the graded weights are available and
could drive the FAIL rule directly, which would remove the proxy. For SaCas9 and
Cas12a they are correctly withheld, so the binary seed is the only available
mechanism and keeping one rule for all nucleases has real value in
comprehensibility. I would keep the binary seed at 12 and record the reasoning
above in the docstring, which is stronger than what is there now. Owner: WP-05.

### 5.5 WP-05's other judgment calls, ruled on

**Call 1, `KNOCKOUT_CDS_MAX_FRACTION = 0.60`.** WP-05 calls this its weakest
number and says it could not source it. **RULING: SOUND as a default, correctly
labelled, and I cannot source it either.** I looked for a published value and
there is no consensus one: design tools genuinely disagree, some preferring the
earliest exons and some an interior window such as the middle 10 to 60 percent of
the transcript. The underlying biology supports a bound of roughly this size for
a real reason, which the docstring states correctly: a frameshift introduced very
near the 3' end can leave a largely intact protein, and a cut in the last exon
can escape nonsense-mediated decay entirely. 0.60 keeps the cut far enough from
the 3' end to make a functional truncation unlikely while not rejecting most of a
short coding sequence. What makes this acceptable is the structure around it
rather than the number: it is labelled `convention`, it is one field on
`GuideRNAThresholds`, the final-exon rule and the constitutive-region rule come
from section 8.5 and are applied independently of it, and check 7 is a WARN. I
would not change it. **UNSOURCED in the strict sense, but correctly declared as
such, which is what section 3.3 constraint 1 requires.** It is not a package
failure because it is not presented as sourced.

**Call 2, check 7 PASS for a non-knockout intent. SOUND.** For `knockin`,
`activation` and `interference` the knockout positioning rule does not exist. PASS
with the reason stated is right and UNKNOWN would be wrong: nothing failed to
evaluate, the constraint is simply absent. UNKNOWN is correctly reserved for the
knockout-with-no-CDS-annotation case, which genuinely cannot be evaluated.

**Call 3, check 7 PASSes when `constitutive_regions` is absent, stating that
isoform coverage was not evaluated. SOUND but the weakest of the three.** Two of
three rules did run, so making the whole check UNKNOWN would hide real
information. The risk is that a user reads PASS and does not read the sentence
saying isoform coverage was not evaluated, and a guide in an alternatively
spliced exon is a real way to fail a knockout. Since the sentence is in the
message and `parameters_used` records it, I accept the call. **Recommendation:
make the partial-evaluation statement the first clause of the message rather than
a trailing one.** Owner: WP-05, cosmetic.

**Call 4, check 8 requires a valid PAM beside the spacer match. SOUND and
necessary.** A guide expression cassette always contains its own spacer, so a
spacer-only search would FAIL every design. Requiring a PAM is what makes the
check mean "the construct would be cut" rather than "the construct contains the
spacer". The true negative is real: in pX330 and derivatives the spacer is
followed by the scaffold beginning `GTTTT`, which is not an NGG PAM, so there is
no self-cut. Asserting both the true positive and that true negative is the
right test design.

**Call 5, check 8 WARNs on a construct near-match with a fully matched seed.
SOUND.** Not required by section 8.5, which names only FAIL, but the reasoning is
good: the construct is at high copy number after transfection, so a seed-matched
near site is a real risk. It is a WARN, so it never turns a usable guide into a
rejected one. Note this WARN inherits the `seed_region_nt = 12` permissiveness
from 5.4.

**Call 6, `NAG` is WARN and never PASS. SOUND and spec-mandated.** Appendix C
says to treat `NAG` as a WARN-level alternative, not a primary PAM. The
consequence, that every NAG guide carries at least a WARN and sorts below primary
guides, is the intended behaviour and not a side effect.

**Call 8, the off-target search requires a PAM beside every reported hit.
SOUND.** A protospacer match with no PAM is not cut. The residual risk WP-05
names is correct and correctly bounded: a non-canonical PAM the nuclease
tolerates but Appendix C does not list would be missed, `NAG` is covered and
nothing beyond it is. That limitation is inherent in Appendix C being the
authority, and the alternative, enumerating speculative PAMs, would be inventing
biology.

**Call 10, only the top-ranked candidate pool is off-target searched. SOUND,
because of the wording.** Guides outside the pool are reported as "not searched"
and never as clean. That is the whole of what matters here. The pool size,
`max(30, 4 x max_guides_returned)`, is an engineering parameter and is correctly
labelled as not biological.

**Section 12 of WP-05, the two remaining third-party identifiers. RULING: keep

### 5.6 The codon table decision (section 7.6, Appendix E)

### 5.7 WP-04 judgment call 4 and check 18: the brief's premise is inverted

The brief asks me to challenge the claim that
`assembly.fragment_order_defined` is **not** applied to simple PCR cloning.
**That premise is incorrect, and I am recording the correction because a fix
built on it would make things worse.**

`APPLICABLE_CHECKS["pcr_cloning"]` at `checks.py` line 74 is
`_UNIVERSAL + ("assembly.fragment_order_defined",)`. Check 18 **is** applied to
PCR cloning. WP-04's own judgment call 4 states the actual behaviour correctly:
"simple PCR cloning with one enzyme at both ends fails check 18, which is
correct, since the insert really can go in either orientation."

The one case where check 18 is skipped is structural, not strategy-based:
`check_fragment_order_defined` returns `None` when `len(junctions) < 2`, because a
single junction cannot be ambiguous. That is correct and is probably the source
of the confusion.

**So the real question is whether FAILing a single-enzyme PCR cloning design is
right. My ruling: the finding is biologically true, the FAIL severity is
spec-mandated, and the message is wrong for this strategy.**

- **The biology is true.** An insert cut with the same enzyme at both ends,
  ligated into a vector cut with that enzyme, genuinely inserts in either
  orientation and genuinely yields a mixture. Reporting it is correct.
- **FAIL is what section 7.5 check 18 specifies** ("FAIL if ambiguous"), so the
  severity is not WP-04's choice.
- **But single-enzyme cloning is a routine, legitimate technique**, not a broken
  design. You do it, then you screen colonies by diagnostic digest or sequencing
  and pick the right orientation. Telling a user their design "cannot be relied on
  to produce the intended order" with no mention of that workflow risks the same
  section 3.4 erosion as the hairpin WARN, and a Tier A known-good PCR cloning
  case would FAIL.
- **The message's two suggested remedies do not apply to this strategy.** "Move
  the junction position" and "supply a published overhang standard whose set is
  distinct by construction" are right for Golden Gate and Gibson. For PCR cloning
  the two real remedies are: use **two different enzymes**, one at each end, which
  makes the cloning directional and is the standard fix; or accept the mixture and
  screen for orientation.

**Recommendation:** keep check 18 applicable to `pcr_cloning` and keep FAIL;
branch the message on strategy so the PCR cloning case names those two remedies.
This is a message change, not a logic change. Owner: WP-04.

All of the following I rule **SOUND**:

### 8.2 Both ERRORs in full

**ERROR 1. The sliding-window hairpin thresholds are miscalibrated by roughly
2 bp, and produce FAILs on correct designs.**

- File: `packages/validation/assembly/constants.py` lines 184 and 190.
- Values found: `HAIRPIN_WINDOW_STEM_WARN_BP = 4`,
  `HAIRPIN_WINDOW_STEM_THREE_PRIME_FAIL_BP = 5`.
- Values expected: **6** and **7**.
- Source contradicted: the nearest-neighbor thermodynamic framework that the
  companion threshold `HAIRPIN_DG_WARN_KCAL_PER_MOL = -2.0` rests on. A 4 bp stem
  closing a 3 nt loop has a folding free energy of about **+0.6 kcal/mol** at
  37 C (three stacks at about -1.3 each, against a triloop penalty of about
  +4.5), so it does not fold at all, and a PCR annealing step at 50 to 72 C is
  more unfavourable still. 6 bp is the stem length whose folding free energy
  equals the -2.0 kcal/mol WARN; 7 bp is the -3.0 kcal/mol FAIL level.
- Measured consequence: on random 47 nt oligos the current WARN fires at
  **0.90** and the current 3' FAIL condition occurs at **0.134**. So roughly nine
  in ten tailed assembly primers draw an unactionable WARN and roughly one in
  seven risks an outright FAIL, on designs with nothing wrong with them. Full
  measurement table in section 2.2.
- Owner: WP-04. Full reasoning and the complete recommendation in section 2.

**ERROR 2. A 3'-anchored hairpin FAIL can be masked by a longer internal stem, so
a FAIL-severity check silently does not fire.**

- File: `packages/validation/assembly/structure.py`, `window_hairpin`, lines
  248-275, with the selection rule at line 264.
- Value found: a single `HairpinResult` selected by maximum stem length, with 3'
  involvement breaking ties only at **equal** stem length:
  `stem > best.stem_bp or (stem == best.stem_bp and involved and not
  best.three_prime_involved)`.
- Value expected: the severity-maximising result. The longest 3'-involved stem
  must be reported whenever it reaches the FAIL threshold, regardless of whether
  some internal stem is longer.
- Source contradicted: the specification's own section 7.5 check 6, which
  requires FAIL when the 3' end lies in a stem at or past the FAIL threshold.
- Measured consequence: at 47 nt, **9 of 161** true 3'-anchored FAIL conditions
  are not reported; at 60 nt, **25 of 192**, which is 13 percent of true FAILs
  missed. Full table in section 2.4.
- This is a **false negative on a FAIL-severity check**, so it is the opposite
  problem to ERROR 1 and fixing the thresholds does not fix it. Under the
  recommended 6/7 thresholds the masking rate falls to zero in 1,200 replicates
  at every length tested, but the defect stays latent.
- Owner: WP-04.

### 8.4 The question: could a design produced by this tool today waste a
researcher's bench time or a production run?

Asked bluntly, so answered bluntly.

**Yes, in two specific ways, and no, not through a wrong number.**

What would **not** happen. I found no wrong constant, no miscited source and no
fabricated sequence anywhere in scope. The enzyme table that would silently
produce a non-assembling construct is correct in all fifteen of its fields. The
Cas12a 5' PAM, which the specification itself names as the most likely
integration bug, is correct and is structurally protected against regression. The
part registry is real sequence from named accessions with a re-fetch verification
path, and no length was massaged toward the planning table. The AAV band, the
ITR handling, the thermodynamics, the two scoring models and their validity
domains are all right. The claim-safety discipline is genuinely better than the
specification demands, and the refusals, no bundled overhang standard, no codon
table, no invented transgene, no MIT score outside SpCas9, are all the correct
calls. On the axis this review was most worried about, a number recalled instead
of read, **this codebase is clean.**

The two real exposures are both about **severity being wrong**, not about biology
being wrong.

**First, and this is the one that costs a production run: the D-outward ITR pair
passes.** A user can hand the tool an ITR arrangement that will not resolve
correctly, and get a PASS with a soft note. AAV production is weeks and real
money. The validator's job is to stop exactly that, and here it does not. It is
one check-severity change to fix.

**Second, and this is the one that costs trust rather than reagents: the hairpin
check is both too loud and, for a FAIL, partly deaf.** Nine in ten tailed
assembly primers draw a WARN the user cannot act on, because a tailed primer
cannot be moved off its junction. About one in seven risks a FAIL on a correct
design. That combination is the section 3.4 failure mode in its pure form: it
trains the user to ignore the validator, and a user who has learned to click
past `primer.hairpin` will also click past the `gg.enzyme_site_internal` FAIL
that genuinely matters. And underneath the noise, ERROR 2 means the one hairpin
that really does stop a primer working, a stem sequestering the 3' terminus, can
be masked by a longer harmless internal stem. So the check is simultaneously
crying wolf and missing the wolf. Neither half is a number read wrongly; both are
a threshold calibrated for a 20-mer and applied to a 47-mer, plus a selection
rule that optimises the wrong quantity.

A third item is not a bench risk but will cost credibility in the room: **the
Kozak artifact**. The tool composes AAV cassettes with no initiation-context
element, so the -3 base is whatever a curator's feature boundary happened to
land on, and only three of eight promoters pass by coin flip, dropping to zero
if the optional intron is included. The validator is right and the composer is
incomplete. A cassette exported as orderable DNA that is missing an element
essentially every real AAV transgene cassette contains is a bad thing to be asked
about, and the fix is fully sourced and small.

**Net.** Nothing here makes me say stop. Fix the two hairpin defects and the
D-outward severity, name the upstream element in the Kozak message, and this is
a validator I would be comfortable putting a real design through. Section 14.4
requires zero UNRESOLVED disagreements rather than zero findings: all eleven
findings above are resolved in the sense that each has a named owner, a specific
value or rule to change, and the reasoning to justify it. None of them is a
disagreement about the biology. They are a disagreement about where two
thresholds and one severity should sit, and I have given a number for each.
