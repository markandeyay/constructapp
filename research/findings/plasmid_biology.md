# Track A -- Plasmid biology & structure findings

## Scope
This track investigated plasmid anatomy and structure for the build: the roles and common placement of origin of replication, promoter, gene of interest, selectable marker, multiple cloning site, and terminator/polyadenylation elements; circular/supercoiled topology; and high-level rules that distinguish a biologically plausible construct from an invalid one.

## What the literature/sources establish
- A cloning plasmid acts as a vector because an inserted DNA fragment can be propagated in a host cell when the plasmid retains replicative ability; Brown describes gene cloning as inserting DNA into a plasmid or virus chromosome that is then replicated in a bacterial or eukaryotic host. [Source: Brown, 2002, "Studying DNA", NCBI Bookshelf, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK21129/]
- Bacterial plasmid propagation depends on an origin of replication recognized by host replication machinery; Brown states that plasmids replicate efficiently in bacterial hosts because each plasmid has an origin of replication recognized by polymerases and proteins that replicate bacterial chromosomes. [Source: Brown, 2002, "Studying DNA", NCBI Bookshelf, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK21129/]
- Origins are not generic labels: del Solar et al. identify plasmid origin(s), often Rep initiator proteins, and replication-control genes as key elements in circular bacterial plasmid replication systems. [Source: del Solar et al., 1998, "Replication and Control of Circular Bacterial Plasmids", DOI: 10.1128/MMBR.62.2.434-464.1998]
- Plasmid vector classification can use replication origin, selection marker, and promoter as core criteria, which supports treating those elements as first-class structured annotations rather than free text. [Source: Wang et al., 2009, "Classification of plasmid vectors using replication origin, selection marker and promoter as criteria", DOI: 10.1016/j.plasmid.2008.09.003]
- Practical plasmid maps usually include an origin of replication, antibiotic-resistance gene, selectable marker, promoter, restriction sites, insert, and primer-binding sites; Addgene's reference map presents those as general plasmid features. [Source: Addgene, 2025, "Molecular Biology Reference", https://www.addgene.org/mol-bio-reference/]
- A bacterial antibiotic-resistance gene is a selectable marker for plasmid-bearing bacteria; Brown gives pBR322 ampicillin and tetracycline resistance as selectable markers because cells carrying the plasmid survive on those antibiotics. [Source: Brown, 2002, "Studying DNA", NCBI Bookshelf, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK21129/]
- A non-bacterial experimental selectable marker can be separate from the bacterial propagation marker; Addgene distinguishes bacterial antibiotic resistance used for plasmid-bearing bacteria from selectable markers used to select experimental target cells, often another antibiotic-resistance gene under a non-bacterial promoter or a fluorescent protein. [Source: Addgene, 2025, "Molecular Biology Reference", https://www.addgene.org/mol-bio-reference/]
- A multiple cloning site is a short DNA segment containing several restriction-enzyme sites for insertion by digestion and ligation, and Addgene notes that MCS restriction sites are generally unique and absent elsewhere in the backbone. [Source: Addgene, 2025, "Molecular Biology Reference", https://www.addgene.org/mol-bio-reference/]
- The classic pBR322 cloning vehicle illustrates backbone validity rules: Bolivar et al. designed pBR322 with antibiotic-resistance genes, a single PstI site in the ampicillin-resistance gene, and unique EcoRI, HindIII, BamHI, and SalI restriction sites. [Source: Bolivar et al., 1977, "Construction and characterization of new cloning vehicles. II. A multipurpose cloning system", DOI: 10.1016/0378-1119(77)90000-2]
- Promoters drive transcription of the insert by recruiting transcriptional machinery; Addgene states that promoter sequence controls RNA polymerase/transcription-factor binding and therefore affects where and when a gene of interest is expressed. [Source: Morgan/Addgene, 2014, "Plasmids 101: The Promoter Region - Let's Go!", https://blog.addgene.org/plasmids-101-the-promoter-region]
- Promoter-host compatibility is a validity requirement; Addgene states that bacterial promoters work in prokaryotic cells and generally in the same or related species, while mammalian, yeast, and plant systems require different promoter classes with little crossover. [Source: Morgan/Addgene, 2014, "Plasmids 101: The Promoter Region - Let's Go!", https://blog.addgene.org/plasmids-101-the-promoter-region]
- Promoter-transcript compatibility is also required; Addgene states that mRNA expression in eukaryotic systems requires an RNA polymerase II promoter, while small RNAs such as shRNA are transcribed from RNA polymerase III promoters. [Source: Morgan/Addgene, 2014, "Plasmids 101: The Promoter Region - Let's Go!", https://blog.addgene.org/plasmids-101-the-promoter-region]
- In expression plasmids, the MCS is often placed downstream from a promoter so the inserted gene can be expressed; Addgene states that expression vectors must contain a promoter, transcription terminator, and inserted gene. [Source: Addgene, 2025, "Molecular Biology Reference", https://www.addgene.org/mol-bio-reference/]
- A typical mammalian expression cassette can be represented as promoter/ORF/polyadenylation; Molloy et al. describe a DNA-vaccine plasmid with a kanamycin-resistance cassette, pUC origin, and mammalian expression cassette consisting of a Pol II promoter, ORF, and polyadenylation element. [Source: Molloy et al., 2004, "Effective and robust plasmid topology analysis and the subsequent characterization of the plasmid isoforms thereby observed", DOI: 10.1093/nar/gnh124]
- For a coding gene of interest, orientation and reading frame matter when the design includes protein fusion tags or cloning into an expression cassette; Addgene's Gateway cloning guidance warns to include expression elements such as ribosome-recognition sequences, start codon, stop codons, and reading-frame considerations. [Source: Addgene, 2016, "Plasmids 101: Gateway Cloning", https://blog.addgene.org/plasmids-101-gateway-cloning]
- Engineered plasmids are commonly propagated in E. coli and are often kept small for manipulation; Addgene states that many common recombinant-DNA plasmids are replicated in E. coli and are relatively small, roughly 3,000 to 6,000 bp, to enable easy manipulation. [Source: Addgene, 2025, "Molecular Biology Reference", https://www.addgene.org/mol-bio-reference/]
- Circular plasmid DNA propagated in E. coli is predominantly negatively supercoiled, while purification/storage can create open-circular and linear forms by single-strand or double-strand nicking; Molloy et al. report supercoiled, relaxed closed-circular, open-circular, linear, multimeric, and aggregate plasmid species in purified batches. [Source: Molloy et al., 2004, "Effective and robust plasmid topology analysis and the subsequent characterization of the plasmid isoforms thereby observed", DOI: 10.1093/nar/gnh124]
- Closed circular plasmid topology is biologically relevant because supercoiling, catenation, and knotting are physical states DNA molecules can adopt; Higgins and Vologodskii review plasmid topological behavior and note that closed circular DNA can be supercoiled. [Source: Higgins and Vologodskii, 2015, "Topological Behavior of Plasmid DNA", DOI: 10.1128/microbiolspec.PLAS-0036-2014]
- Viral-vector transfer plasmids add vector-type-specific requirements beyond the generic plasmid backbone; Addgene describes lentiviral and retroviral transfer plasmids as carrying cargo while helper plasmids supply packaging or replication components. [Source: Addgene, 2023, "Viral Vectors 101: Viral Vector Elements", https://blog.addgene.org/viral-vectors-101-viral-vector-elements]

## What this means for our build
- The canonical annotation model should require separate feature types for `ORI`, `promoter`, `GOI`, `marker`, `MCS`, `terminator`, and `other`, because those elements are repeatedly identified as distinct functional units in plasmid references. [Source: Addgene, 2025, "Molecular Biology Reference", https://www.addgene.org/mol-bio-reference/; Source: Wang et al., 2009, "Classification of plasmid vectors using replication origin, selection marker and promoter as criteria", DOI: 10.1016/j.plasmid.2008.09.003]
- The parser should distinguish bacterial propagation features from experimental expression features, because a valid mammalian expression plasmid may need both an E. coli origin/antibiotic marker and a mammalian promoter/expression marker. [Source: Brown, 2002, "Studying DNA", NCBI Bookshelf, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK21129/; Source: Addgene, 2025, "Molecular Biology Reference", https://www.addgene.org/mol-bio-reference/]
- A generated expression cassette should normally place the promoter upstream of the GOI/MCS and a terminator or polyadenylation element downstream of the expressed sequence, because expression vectors are described as promoter plus inserted gene plus transcription terminator, and mammalian cassettes are described as promoter/ORF/polyadenylation. [Source: Addgene, 2025, "Molecular Biology Reference", https://www.addgene.org/mol-bio-reference/; Source: Molloy et al., 2004, "Effective and robust plasmid topology analysis and the subsequent characterization of the plasmid isoforms thereby observed", DOI: 10.1093/nar/gnh124]
- The validity engine should fail constructs with no compatible origin for the intended propagation host, no selectable marker for the intended selection context, no compatible promoter for the intended expression host/transcript, no downstream terminator/polyadenylation for expression, or an inserted GOI that disrupts an essential backbone function. [Source: Brown, 2002, "Studying DNA", NCBI Bookshelf, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK21129/; Source: Morgan/Addgene, 2014, "Plasmids 101: The Promoter Region - Let's Go!", https://blog.addgene.org/plasmids-101-the-promoter-region; Source: Addgene, 2025, "Molecular Biology Reference", https://www.addgene.org/mol-bio-reference/]
- The design representation should store `topology` separately from sequence text, because the same nucleotide sequence can be represented as circular, linearized, open-circular, or supercoiled physical species in plasmid handling and manufacturing contexts. [Source: Molloy et al., 2004, "Effective and robust plasmid topology analysis and the subsequent characterization of the plasmid isoforms thereby observed", DOI: 10.1093/nar/gnh124; Source: Higgins and Vologodskii, 2015, "Topological Behavior of Plasmid DNA", DOI: 10.1128/microbiolspec.PLAS-0036-2014]
- Retrieval and generation should treat vector type as a constraint, not just a label, because viral transfer plasmids and standard cloning/expression plasmids have different required elements and helper-system assumptions. [Source: Addgene, 2023, "Viral Vectors 101: Viral Vector Elements", https://blog.addgene.org/viral-vectors-101-viral-vector-elements; Source: Addgene, 2025, "Molecular Biology Reference", https://www.addgene.org/mol-bio-reference/]

## Decisions proposed
- Use this minimum "generic expression plasmid" structural expectation for Phase 0/Phase 3 checks: circular annotated sequence; bacterial propagation module with compatible `ORI` plus bacterial selection marker; expression module with host-compatible `promoter -> GOI/MCS -> terminator/polyA`; optional experimental selectable/reporter marker; and vector-type-specific extras for viral or specialized vectors. [Source: Brown, 2002, "Studying DNA", NCBI Bookshelf, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK21129/; Source: Addgene, 2025, "Molecular Biology Reference", https://www.addgene.org/mol-bio-reference/; Source: Molloy et al., 2004, "Effective and robust plasmid topology analysis and the subsequent characterization of the plasmid isoforms thereby observed", DOI: 10.1093/nar/gnh124]
- Store feature coordinates with strand/orientation and validate ordering on the relevant strand, because promoter-upstream and terminator-downstream rules only make sense relative to the transcribed GOI orientation. [Source: Morgan/Addgene, 2014, "Plasmids 101: The Promoter Region - Let's Go!", https://blog.addgene.org/plasmids-101-the-promoter-region; Source: Addgene, 2025, "Molecular Biology Reference", https://www.addgene.org/mol-bio-reference/]
- Treat MCS uniqueness and internal restriction-site conflicts as validation checks parameterized by cloning method, because MCS utility depends on usable restriction sites and pBR322-style vectors were explicitly designed around unique restriction sites. [Source: Addgene, 2025, "Molecular Biology Reference", https://www.addgene.org/mol-bio-reference/; Source: Bolivar et al., 1977, "Construction and characterization of new cloning vehicles. II. A multipurpose cloning system", DOI: 10.1016/0378-1119(77)90000-2]
- Represent bacterial marker and experimental target-cell marker as separate concepts in schemas and UI, because Addgene distinguishes the bacterial antibiotic-resistance feature from markers used to select or sort experimental cells. [Source: Addgene, 2025, "Molecular Biology Reference", https://www.addgene.org/mol-bio-reference/]

## Open questions / conflicts
- "Valid construct" is vector-type-specific: generic plasmid rules do not define all required lentiviral, AAV, CRISPR, shRNA, yeast, plant, or bacterial-expression elements, so later tracks or follow-up research should define per-vector-type validity profiles. [Source: Addgene, 2023, "Viral Vectors 101: Viral Vector Elements", https://blog.addgene.org/viral-vectors-101-viral-vector-elements; Source: Addgene, 2025, "Molecular Biology Reference", https://www.addgene.org/mol-bio-reference/]
- The minimum acceptable terminator/polyadenylation annotation differs by host and transcript type, because Addgene describes expression vectors generally as requiring a transcription terminator while Molloy et al. describe a mammalian Pol II cassette with polyadenylation. [Source: Addgene, 2025, "Molecular Biology Reference", https://www.addgene.org/mol-bio-reference/; Source: Molloy et al., 2004, "Effective and robust plasmid topology analysis and the subsequent characterization of the plasmid isoforms thereby observed", DOI: 10.1093/nar/gnh124]
- Size guidance is contextual: Addgene notes many commonly used E. coli-propagated recombinant plasmids are roughly 3,000 to 6,000 bp for easy manipulation, but viral vectors, BACs, complex multi-cassette constructs, and synthesis-provider constraints require separate size rules. [Source: Addgene, 2025, "Molecular Biology Reference", https://www.addgene.org/mol-bio-reference/; Source: Brown, 2002, "Studying DNA", NCBI Bookshelf, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK21129/]
- Plasmid topology matters for physical manufacturing and QC, but a sequence generator can only specify circular versus linear sequence topology unless later workflow stages model preparation state such as supercoiled, open-circular, or linear isoform. [Source: Molloy et al., 2004, "Effective and robust plasmid topology analysis and the subsequent characterization of the plasmid isoforms thereby observed", DOI: 10.1093/nar/gnh124; Source: Higgins and Vologodskii, 2015, "Topological Behavior of Plasmid DNA", DOI: 10.1128/microbiolspec.PLAS-0036-2014]

## 2026-05-29 update: profile-aware completeness
- The Phase 0 parser no longer treats `ORI + promoter + marker + terminator` as a universal completeness rule. That rule is appropriate for many expression plasmids, but it incorrectly marks valid cloning backbones and base shuttle vectors incomplete because those vectors are designed to receive an expression cassette later rather than carry a complete one at the backbone stage. [Source: Yanisch-Perron et al., 1985, "Improved M13 phage cloning vectors and host strains: nucleotide sequences of the M13mp18 and pUC19 vectors", DOI: 10.1016/0378-1119(85)90120-9; Source: Sikorski and Hieter, 1989, "A system of shuttle vectors and yeast host strains designed for efficient manipulation of DNA in Saccharomyces cerevisiae", DOI: 10.1093/genetics/122.1.19]
- The implemented Phase 0 profiles are `bacterial_cloning_vector`, `bacterial_expression_vector`, `mammalian_expression_vector`, `mammalian_reporter_vector`, `lentiviral_or_retroviral_transfer_vector`, `crispr_vector`, `yeast_shuttle_vector`, `general_shuttle_vector`, and `unknown`. These are intentionally coarse: they separate the major validity regimes without creating an unreviewable taxonomy of every vendor family. [Source: Wang et al., 2009, "Classification of plasmid vectors using replication origin, selection marker and promoter as criteria", DOI: 10.1016/j.plasmid.2008.09.003; Source: Addgene, 2025, "Molecular Biology Reference", https://www.addgene.org/mol-bio-reference/]
- Profile-aware completeness rules now require only the elements that are structurally expected for the assigned profile. For example, bacterial cloning vectors require a bacterial origin, selectable marker, and MCS or equivalent cloning/marker-disruption structure; mammalian expression vectors require a mammalian promoter and terminator/polyA around a GOI or cloning slot; reporter vectors require a reporter payload rather than a separate non-reporter GOI; yeast shuttle vectors require bacterial propagation plus yeast maintenance/selection rather than a mammalian-style expression cassette. [Source: Thermo Fisher pcDNA3.1 documentation, https://www.thermofisher.com/order/catalog/product/V79020; Source: Promega pGL3 luciferase reporter vector manual, https://www.promega.com/resources/protocols/technical-manuals/0/pgl3-luciferase-reporter-vectors-protocol/; Source: Promega pGL4 luciferase reporter vector documentation, https://www.promega.com/products/reporter-assays-and-transfection/reporter-vectors-and-cell-lines/pgl4-luciferase-reporter-vectors/; Source: Sikorski and Hieter, 1989, DOI: 10.1093/genetics/122.1.19]
- Unclassifiable plasmids are assigned the `unknown` profile and `annotation_complete=false`. They are not rejected, because they may still be useful for inspection or retrieval, but they are excluded from training-eligible complete records until a reviewable profile-specific rule applies. This is a conservative data-quality policy rather than a biological claim that the plasmid is invalid. [Source: Addgene, 2025, "Molecular Biology Reference", https://www.addgene.org/mol-bio-reference/]
- Residual questions remain for exact regulatory-element provenance in viral, CRISPR, and some yeast-expression vectors. The reference library should not add U6, PuroR, lentiviral LTR/Psi/RRE/cPPT/WPRE, sgRNA scaffold, Cas9, BGH polyA, EF1a, HygR, BSD, ZeoR, GAL1/GPD/CYC1, or 2-micron sequences until exact public-source coordinates and licensing/provenance are resolved.

## 2026-05-30 appendix: profile-boundary edge cases

### Established facts from authoritative sources
- A cloning backbone and an expression vector are not interchangeable categories. Addgene describes expression plasmids as requiring a promoter, transcription terminator, and inserted gene, while cloning backbones provide propagation and insertion machinery such as an origin, selectable marker, and MCS. [Source: Addgene, "Molecular Biology Reference", https://www.addgene.org/mol-bio-reference/]
- SP6, T7, and T3 promoter annotations do not establish an in-vivo expression purpose by themselves. Promega documents pGEM cloning vectors with SP6 and T7 promoters flanking the MCS for RNA synthesis and sequencing. Agilent documents pBluescript II cloning vectors with T3 and T7 promoters flanking the polylinker for in-vitro RNA transcription and sequencing-primer access. [Source: Promega, "pGEM-T and pGEM-T Easy Vector Systems Technical Manual", https://www.promega.com/-/media/files/resources/protocols/technical-manuals/0/pgem-t-and-pgem-t-easy-vector-systems-protocol.pdf; Source: Agilent, "pBluescript II Phagemid Vectors Instruction Manual", https://www.agilent.com/cs/library/usermanuals/public/212205.pdf]
- T7 can be part of a bacterial expression system when corroborating context is present. NEB describes T7 expression vectors as placing the target gene downstream of a T7 promoter and using a host that supplies T7 RNA polymerase; Merck's pET manual describes T7lac variants that add a lac operator downstream of the T7 promoter for regulated expression. [Source: NEB, "Protein Expression with T7 Express Strains", https://www.neb.com/en-us/tools-and-resources/feature-articles/protein-expression-with-t7-express-strains; Source: Merck, "pET System Manual", https://www.merckmillipore.com/INTERSHOP/web/WFS/Merck-US-Site/en_US/-/USD/ShowDocument-File?ProductSKU=EMD_BIO-71827&DocumentId=TB055]
- Shuttle vectors are defined by replication or maintenance across hosts, not automatically by an expression cassette. Sikorski and Hieter describe pRS yeast shuttle vectors designed for efficient DNA manipulation in *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*, with yeast selectable markers and replication/maintenance elements on bacterial-propagated plasmids. [Source: Sikorski and Hieter, 1989, "A system of shuttle vectors and yeast host strains designed for efficient manipulation of DNA in Saccharomyces cerevisiae", DOI: 10.1093/genetics/122.1.19]
- Functional categories can overlap. Ran et al. describe CRISPR plasmids that combine Cas9 and sgRNA expression, including a GFP-containing variant for enrichment of transfected cells. Sanjana et al. describe lentiviral CRISPR vectors with Cas9 and sgRNA components, showing that delivery modality and functional purpose can both be biologically relevant. [Source: Ran et al., 2013, "Genome engineering using the CRISPR-Cas9 system", DOI: 10.1038/nprot.2013.143; Source: Sanjana et al., 2014, "Improved vectors and genome-wide libraries for CRISPR screening", DOI: 10.1038/nmeth.3047]

### Build inferences for explicit classifier rules
- Keep a cloning or cloning-shuttle profile unless there is explicit expression-cassette evidence. A sequencing-class SP6, T7, or T3 promoter plus generic CDS annotations is insufficient. For bacterial expression, require stronger context such as a host-appropriate promoter with an oriented payload and corroboration such as lacO regulation, an RBS, an affinity tag, or a terminator. This is a conservative classifier policy derived from the source distinctions above, not a claim that every valid expression vector has the same cassette.
- Treat T7 as context-sensitive: `T7` alone is sequencing/in-vitro-transcription evidence; `T7 + lacO` or `T7 + oriented expression payload + bacterial-expression context` can support `bacterial_expression_vector`.
- Classify shuttle capability orthogonally where possible. A yeast shuttle backbone without an expression cassette remains `yeast_shuttle_vector`; a multi-host vector with a reviewed expression cassette may need both a host-capability modifier and a primary functional profile.
- Prefer functional purpose over accessory reporters for multifunction constructs. A Cas9/sgRNA plasmid carrying GFP for enrichment should remain `crispr_vector` with a reporter modifier. A lentiviral CRISPR transfer plasmid should remain `crispr_vector` with a lentiviral-delivery modifier once lentiviral hallmarks are confirmed. This precedence rule is a build inference intended to preserve the construct's experimental purpose.
- Require token-boundary or structured-feature matching for short signals such as `LTR`, `T3`, `T7`, `SP6`, and marker aliases. Short substring matches are not biologically meaningful and can turn unrelated feature names into false profile evidence.

### Unresolved biology and product-policy questions
- A future schema should decide whether profile modifiers are first-class fields. Flattening `crispr_vector + lentiviral_delivery + reporter` into one label loses biologically useful distinctions, but a modifier model needs explicit downstream retrieval and validation semantics.
- A reviewed rule is still needed for promoterless reporter backbones: they are legitimately reporter vectors even when an experimental promoter is intentionally absent for insertion during cloning.
- Plant, insect, and cell-free expression profiles remain outside the current Phase 0 taxonomy. Their host-specific promoters, selection systems, and replication assumptions should be researched before those records become training-eligible.

## 2026-05-31 appendix: conservative shuttle-vector boundary

- A shuttle vector requires replication or maintained propagation across multiple host contexts, not merely multiple `ORI` annotations. Collapse ColE1/pMB1/pBR322/pUC aliases into one bacterial replicon family. Do not count `f1` ssDNA-rescue origins, `oriT` transfer origins, or `CEN` partition elements as independent cellular replication support. [Source: Holmes and Jobling, 1996, "Genetics: A Conceptual Approach", NCBI Bookshelf, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK7908/#A1426; Source: Agilent, "pBluescript II Phagemid Vectors Instruction Manual", https://www.agilent.com/cs/library/usermanuals/public/212205.pdf; Source: Ares-Arroyo et al., 2022, "Origins of transfer establish networks of mobility among plasmids in the human gut microbiome", https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10123127/]
- An explicitly annotated SV40 replication origin is a conditional mammalian replication element because SV40 large T antigen initiates replication at that origin. Generic `SV40` text, SV40 promoters, and SV40 polyadenylation signals are not replication evidence. [Source: Cooper, 2000, "The Cell: A Molecular Approach", NCBI Bookshelf, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK9940/]
- For Phase 0 classification, admit a shuttle profile only from reviewed explicit source metadata or from normalized autonomous replication support for at least two distinct host classes. This deliberately conservative sequence-only rule may defer genuine inter-bacterial shuttle vectors to the metadata lane. [Source: Schweizer, 1991, "Escherichia-Pseudomonas shuttle vectors derived from pUC18/19", DOI: 10.1016/0378-1119(91)90016-5]
- Yeast integrating vectors can be called shuttle vectors despite lacking autonomous yeast replication. Keep those metadata-admitted but incomplete until an integrating-vector profile exists. [Source: Sikorski and Hieter, 1989, "A system of shuttle vectors and yeast host strains designed for efficient manipulation of DNA in Saccharomyces cerevisiae", DOI: 10.1093/genetics/122.1.19]
